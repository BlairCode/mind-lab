\title{\textbf{Waltz of Skill and Show: From Latent Reconstruction to Hybrid System Optimization}}

\begin{document}

% -------------- 摘要页 --------------
\begin{abstract}
{
\setlength{\parskip}{0.15em} 
\setstretch{1.1}

The elimination mechanism of \textit{Dancing with the Stars} is essentially a multi-source heterogeneous evaluation system that integrates judges' scores and audience preferences. However, due to the long-term absence of audience voting data, the operational logic of this system has always been in a black box state.

To address the unobservability of latent fan votes, we establish a \textbf{Hybrid Inference Architecture}. By synergizing \textbf{Neural-Guided Variational Inference} with \textbf{Monte Carlo Rejection Sampling}, we transform rigid elimination rules into hard posterior constraints to reconstruct voting distributions under a \textbf{Dirichlet prior}. Experiments demonstrate a \textbf{99.66\% Top-1 reconstruction accuracy} and a high \textbf{Posterior Concentration Index of 0.9864}, validating the feasibility of mathematically deconstructing complex sociological latent variables via constraint-based inference.

A counterfactual simulation framework and fairness metrics are constructed to compare the ranking-based system and the percentage-based system. By additionally analyzing historical exceptional cases and establishing a utility function, the results indicate that the \textbf{ranking-based system can more effectively protect technically skilled competitors}. Using a cost-benefit comparative approach, it is found that the application of rescue mechanisms is subject to \textbf{contingency-based restrictions}.

To quantify the heterogeneous impact of features on competition outcomes, we proposed a \textbf{causal mediation analysis framework}. By combining \textbf{XGBoost with SHAP attribution}, we decomposed the direct and indirect effects of each feature on survival results. Experiments revealed that \textbf{age and dance partner} were the main features influencing the results, and different features had significant differences in their impact on judges versus audiences.

We proposed the \textbf{Fairness-Enhanced Hybrid Scoring System (FEHSS)}, which employs XGBoost models to isolate a contestant’s merit from structural background bias by deriving \textbf{orthogonal performance residuals}. Through a \textbf{time-varying geometric aggregation mechanism}, the system dynamically balances technical and popularity dimensions, thereby mitigating over-reliance on any single metric. Counterfactual simulations demonstrate that FEHSS significantly reduces the correlation between outcomes and protected factors while achieving an \textbf{optimal balance between fairness and entertainment value} within a identified parameter range.

\vspace{0.15cm}
}
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Background}
\vspace{0.1cm}
In the domain of social choice theory, hybrid assessment systems—which aggregate expert evaluation with public opinion—are ubiquitous, governing outcomes in fields ranging from political elections to talent competitions. 

\begin{wrapfigure}{r}{0.42\textwidth}
    \centering
    \vspace{-15pt} % 适当调整上方间距
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{imgs/iceberg.png}

    \label{fig:iceberg}
    \vspace{-6pt} % 适当调整下方间距
\end{wrapfigure}

\textit{Dancing with the Stars}, or DWTS, serves as a quintessential paradigm of such a system, where the final hierarchy is determined by the interplay between ``technical merit'', as quantified by judges, and ``audience appeal'', a latent variable driven by popularity and narrative \cite{1}.


The fundamental mathematical challenge stems from \textbf{information asymmetry}, a dynamic we visually conceptualize as the \textbf{Iceberg Model} (see Figure~\ref{fig:iceberg}). Within this framework, the explicitly observable judges' scores represent only the exposed tip, while the latent fan voting distributions form the massive hidden variable beneath the surface. This structure constitutes an \textbf{Ill-posed Inverse Problem}, requiring the reconstruction of high-dimensional latent fan distributions based solely on sparse binary elimination outcomes.

\subsection{Problem Restatement}
Based on the problem description, we need to address the following tasks:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Reconstruction of Latent Fan Votes.}
    Construct an inverse problem framework to derive latent fan votes consistent with historical eliminations and judge scores. The model must also quantify the estimation's certainty and consistency to validate data reliability.

    \item \textbf{Comparative Analysis of Voting Mechanisms. }
     Contrast Rank-based and Percentage-based aggregations to determine fan sensitivity through counterfactual simulation. Furthermore, evaluate the efficacy and structural necessity of the "Judges’ Save" mechanism.

    \item \textbf{Multidimensional Feature Impact Assessment.}
    Utilize reconstructed fan votes to quantify covariate impacts on outcomes, specifically identifying the divergence in factors driving Technical Merit versus Audience Appeal.

    \item \textbf{Optimization of Scoring Architecture.}
    Design a robust scoring framework that integrates multi-source evaluations and mitigates outliers to maximize fairness and supersede traditional model limitations.

    \item \textbf{Strategic Policy Recommendations.}
    Synthesize the empirical findings into a comprehensive memo for the show's producers. This deliverable will provide actionable and evidence-based advice on the optimal method for combining scores and whether to implement the Judges' Save protocol in future seasons.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Our Work}
Our specific practices are as follows：

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Task 1: Latent Variable Reconstruction.} We propose a Hybrid Inference Architecture that integrates \textbf{Neural-guided Variational Inference} with \textbf{Monte Carlo Rejection Sampling}. This framework utilizes a Dirichlet prior to reconstruct latent fan voting shares, ensuring high consistency and certainty in reproducing historical elimination facts.
    
    \item \textbf{Task 2: Fairness Assessment.} We construct a \textbf{Counterfactual Causal Framework} to contrast Rank-based and Percentage-based systems. By permuting likelihood constraints and calculating the loss, we quantify structural biases and the nonlinear perturbations caused by the Judges' Save mechanism.
    
    \item \textbf{Task 3: Multidimensional Attribution.} A nonlinear ensemble learning framework based on \textbf{XGBoost and SHAP attribution} is established. We disentangle the influence of partner resources, age, and industry background, identifying the divergent drivers behind technical scores versus fan popularity.
    
    \item \textbf{Task 4: System Optimization.} We design a \textbf{Fairness-Enhanced Hybrid Scoring System} incorporating \textbf{Residual Decomposition} to strip away exogenous biases. Furthermore, a \textbf{Time-Varying Geometric Aggregation Operator} is introduced to dynamically modulate the weights between expert merit and fan influence, ensuring long-term competitive integrity.
    
    \item \textbf{Task 5: Memorandum and Implementation Roadmap.} At the end of this article, a data-driven reform plan for the scoring system and an implementation roadmap are provided to the program team, aiming to maximize audience engagement while reshaping the program's long-term credibility and brand value.
\end{itemize}


% -------------- 2. Assumptions --------------
\section{Assumptions}
\begin{itemize}
    \setlength{\itemsep}{3pt}
    
    \item \textbf{Assumption 1: Markovian Popularity.} 
    A contestant’s popularity at week $t$ depends solely on the state at $t-1$ and current performance. 
    
    \textit{Justification:} This reduces model complexity, enabling a tractable first-order recursive Bayesian update framework.

    \item \textbf{Assumption 2: Dirichlet Distribution.} 
    % At any given time $t$, the vote share vector for all contestants, denoted as $\bm{X}_t = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$, follows a Dirichlet distribution parameterized by $\bm{\alpha}_t$.
    The vote share vector $X_t$ follows a Dirichlet distribution parameterized by $\alpha_t$. 
    
    \textit{Justification:} Since vote shares are compositional data constrained to a simplex, the Dirichlet distribution is the rigorous stochastic model for this domain.

    \item \textbf{Assumption 3: Sufficiency of Partner Features.} 
    The extracted historical performance metrics fully characterize a professional partner’s technical and pedagogical competence. 
    
    \textit{Justification:} Utilizing historical proxies significantly reduces feature dimensionality while preserving model interpretability.

    \item \textbf{Assumption 4: Temporal Independence.} 
    Each time step is treated as an independent sample point for fairness and sensitivity analysis, neglecting sequential causal dependencies.
    
    \textit{Justification:} As the discriminative format remains invariant throughout the evaluation, its systemic fairness measures are considered time-independent.

    \item \textbf{Assumption 5: Latent Parameter Stability under Perturbations.} 
    Fan vote prior parameters remain invariant across scoring structures, and posterior samples provide unbiased estimates of original format parameters.
    
    \textit{Justification:} This simplification decouples scoring systems from latent variables, enabling reconstructed data to be used for cross-format comparative analysis.
\end{itemize}

\section{Task 1: Inverse Reconstruction of Latent Fan Votes}
\label{sec:task1}

% \subsection{Problem Analysis and Hybrid Inference Architecture}
Task 1 addresses the partially observable nature of the competition system where Judge Scores $J$ and Elimination Results $E$ are explicit, but Fan Vote Proportions $X$ remain latent. Reconstructing $X$ represents an ill-posed inverse problem governed by three constraints:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Simplex Constraint:} The vote vector $X_t = [x_{1,t}, \dots, x_{n,t}]^T$ strictly adheres to the standard $(n-1)$-simplex satisfying $\sum x_{i,t} = 1$ and $x_{i,t} \ge 0$.
    \item \textbf{Data Sparsity:} Limited historical seasons increase the risk of overfitting when applying high-dimensional regression models directly.
    \item \textbf{Regime Shifts:} The aggregation logic alternates between \textit{Rank-based} and \textit{Percentage-based} systems which dynamically alters the feasible solution space topology.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{-0.2cm}
\subsection{Hybrid Inference Architecture}

To navigate these complexities, we propose a \textbf{Hybrid Inference Architecture} shown in Figure \ref{fig:model_arch}. This framework synergizes \textbf{Neural-Guided Variational Inference (VI)} for global optimization with \textbf{Bayesian Monte Carlo Rejection Sampling} for local density estimation \cite{2}.

\subsubsection{Bayesian Generative Framework}
We model latent fan vote proportions using a \textbf{Dirichlet Distribution}, the natural conjugate prior for multinomial data \cite{3}. For week $t$, the probability density function is:

\vspace{-0.25cm}
\begin{equation}
    p(X_t | \boldsymbol{\alpha}_t) = \frac{1}{B(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_t)} \prod_{i=1}^{n} x_{i,t}^{\alpha_{i,t}-1}, \quad \text{where } \boldsymbol{\alpha}_t = [\alpha_{1,t}, \dots, \alpha_{n,t}]
\end{equation}

\vspace{-0.2cm}
The Dirichlet parameter $\alpha_{i,t}$ quantifies the $i$-th contestant’s latent popularity. Guided by empirical survival analysis (see Figure \ref{fig:survival_analysis1} and \ref{fig:survival_analysis2}), we identified Industry and Age as significant determinants of longevity, utilizing them to initialize the prior state $\alpha_0$.

Addressing high cardinality, we aggregated `Industry' labels into five primary clusters: \textit{Actor/Actress}, \textit{Athlete}, \textit{TV Personality}, \textit{Singer/Rapper}, and \textit{Other}. The initialization function is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_{i,0} = \exp\left( \mathbf{W}_{ind} \cdot \text{OneHot}(\text{Industry}_i) + w_{age} \cdot \text{Age}_i^{norm} \right)
\end{equation}
The exponential function ensures non-negativity. $\mathbf{W}_{ind}$ and $w_{age}$ represent learnable global weights derived in the subsequent optimization phase.

To formalize the non-stationary nature of public sentiment, we implement a \textbf{Recursive Bayesian Filter}. This mechanism propagates information temporally, utilizing the posterior vote estimate from week $t$ as the informative prior for week $t+1$. The state transition follows the linear update equation:
\vspace{-0.1cm}
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\alpha}_{t+1} = \underbrace{\gamma_{dec} \cdot \boldsymbol{\alpha}_{t}}_{\text{Memory Decay}} + \underbrace{K \cdot \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t}}_{\text{Bayesian Innovation}}
\end{equation}

\vspace{-0.2cm}
$\gamma_{dec} \in (0,1)$ is the \textbf{Memory Decay Factor} representing natural fanbase erosion.$K > 0$ denotes the \textbf{Bayesian Innovation Coefficient} or Learning Rate. It quantifies the update magnitude induced by the most recent estimate $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t}$. A larger $K$ implies high elasticity where the prior is acutely sensitive to immediate performance.

\subsubsection{Neural-Guided Variational Inference for Parameter Space}
The hyperparameter set requiring optimization is $\Theta = \{\mathbf{W}_{ind}, w_{age}, \gamma, K\}$. Given data sparsity and high dimensionality, exhaustive methods like Grid Search are computationally intractable. Consequently, we employ \textbf{Neural-Guided Variational Inference (VI)} to approximate the full posterior distribution $p(\Theta | \mathcal{D})$.

Maximizing the marginal likelihood function is equivalent to maximizing the ELBO. The ELBO can be decomposed into two interpretable components:
\vspace{-0.2cm}
\begin{align}
    ELBO &= \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q_{\phi}(\Theta)}[\log p(\mathcal{D}|\Theta)]}_{\text{Reconstruction Likelihood}} - \underbrace{D_{KL}(q_{\phi}(\Theta) || p(\Theta))}_{\text{Regularization Term}}
\end{align}
\vspace{-0.5cm}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Expected Log-Likelihood:} Represents historical reconstruction capacity, optimizing $p(\Theta)$ to align Monte Carlo simulations with actual elimination facts.
    \item \textbf{KL Divergence:} Acts as a regularizer, compressing the search space into a high-confidence feasible region by constraining deviation from the prior.
\end{itemize}

The neural network will output a mean $\mu_{\phi}$ and a variance $\sigma_{\phi}$. To compress the search space, we extract the $\pm2\sigma_{\phi}$ confidence interval and define a compact subspace for heuristic fine-tuning: $\Theta_{search} = [\mu_{\phi} - 2\sigma_{\phi}, \mu_{\phi} + 2\sigma_{\phi}]$

% We implement a lightweight inference network parameterized by $\phi$ to output the mean $\mu_{\phi}$ and variance $\sigma_{\phi}^2$. Training this network to maximize the ELBO yields optimal variational parameters. 

% To contract the parameter space into a feasible region, we extract the $\pm 2\sigma$ confidence interval. This delimits a compact subspace for heuristic fine-tuning: $\Theta_{search} = [\mu_{\phi} - 2\sigma_{\phi}, \mu_{\phi} + 2\sigma_{\phi}]$

This hybrid approach leverages neural network global exploration to guide local optimization, ensuring efficiency and accuracy.

To validate the search space $\Theta_{search}$, we conducted an ablation study sampling parameters outside the guided region. Figure \ref{fig:param_space_impact} shows a strong negative correlation between distance from the optimum and Top-1 Accuracy. \subsubsection{Inverse Reconstruction: Monte Carlo Rejection Sampling}
We employ a rejection sampling strategy to delineate the ``Feasible Polytope,'' the subspace of vote proportions mathematically compatible with observed historical outcomes.

\noindent \textbf{Step 1. Sampling:} 
For week $t$, we draw $N=50,000$ i.i.d. samples from the parameterized Dirichlet prior $X_t^{(m)} \sim \text{Dir}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_t)$.

\noindent \textbf{Step 2. Constraint Filtering:} 
We construct a binary indicator function $\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{x}_t^{(m)})$ to evaluate sample consistency with the historical elimination of contestant $e$.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Scenario A: Percentage Rule.} 
    Validity requires the eliminated contestant $e$ to have a strictly lower total score than any other contestant $i$. The indicator function is:

    \vspace{-0.25cm}
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbb{I}(\mathbf{x}_t^{(m)}) = \begin{cases} 
        1, & \text{if } \forall i \neq e, \left(x_{i,t}^{(m)} + P_{J,i,t}\right) > \left(x_{e,t}^{(m)} + P_{J,e,t}\right) \\ 
        0, & \text{otherwise} 
        \end{cases}
    \end{equation}
    \vspace{-0.25cm}
    
    \item \textbf{Scenario B: Rank Rule.} 
    The composite rank is the summation $R_{Total,i}^{(m)} = R_{Fan,i}^{(m)} + R_{Judge,i}$. A sample is valid if the eliminated contestant $e$ holds the lowest aggregate standing, represented by the maximum total rank:
    \begin{equation}
        \vspace{-0.2cm}
        \mathbb{I}(\mathbf{x}_t^{(m)}) = \begin{cases} 
        1, & \text{if } \arg\max_{j} (R_{Total,j}^{(m)}) = e \\ 
        0, & \text{otherwise} 
        \end{cases}
        \vspace{-0.2cm}
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\noindent \textbf{Step 3. Posterior Estimation:} 
The posterior fan vote estimate $\hat{x}_{i,t}$ is derived by calculating the \textbf{arithmetic mean} of the validated sample set $\mathcal{X}_{valid}$, effectively aggregating all Monte Carlo samples that satisfy the historical elimination constraints.

\subsubsection{Consistency Measures: Reconstructing Historical Truth}
We introduce a hierarchical consistency framework to rigorously evaluate model fidelity in reproducing elimination outcomes.

\noindent \textbf{Level I: Top-1 Accuracy ($\mathcal{A}_{top1}$).} \par
This primary metric assesses if the predicted eliminated contestant corresponds to the ground truth $e_{true,t}$ in week $t$: $\mathcal{A}_{top1} = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \mathbb{I}(\hat{e}_{t} = e_{true,t})$

Figure \ref{fig:heuristic_trajectory} illustrates the optimization trajectory. Under optimal hyperparameters, our model achieves a \textbf{Top-1 Accuracy of 99.66\%}, demonstrating exceptional fidelity.

We also evaluate the probability that the true eliminated contestant falls within the predicted bottom-$k$ set: $\mathcal{A}_{topk} = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \mathbb{I}(e_{true,t} \in \text{Bottom}_k(\text{Predicted Ranks}))$

\subsubsection{Certainty Measures: Quantifying Systemic Ambiguity}
Complementing consistency analysis, we evaluate probabilistic confidence using Posterior Concentration and Information Entropy.

\noindent \textbf{Metric I: Posterior Concentration Index (PCI).} \par
Utilizing posterior variance as a proxy for epistemic uncertainty, the \textbf{Posterior Concentration Index} quantifies solution space compactness:
\begin{equation}
    \vspace{-0.1cm}
    PCI = 1 - \sqrt{\frac{1}{T \cdot N} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \sum_{i=1}^{N_t} \sigma_{i,t}^2}
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
\end{equation}
PCI approaching unity indicates a highly constrained feasible polytope. Figure \ref{fig:pci_season} shows a global average of \textbf{98.65\%}, demonstrating high confidence. Controversial seasons exhibit lower PCI which accurately reflects latent ambiguity.

\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{imgs/PCI.png}
    \caption{\textbf{Posterior Concentration Index (PCI) across Seasons.}}
    \label{fig:pci_season}
\end{figure}

\noindent \textbf{Metric II: Evolution of Information Entropy.} \par
We calculate the Shannon Entropy of the posterior elimination probability to gauge risk dispersion. Let $m_{i,t}$ denote the frequency with which contestant $i$ satisfies the elimination criteria across $N$ simulations; the posterior elimination probability is explicitly defined as the ratio $p_{i,t} = m_{i,t}/N$. Subsequently, the Information Entropy $H_t = - \sum_{i=1}^{n} p_{i,t} \log_2(p_{i,t})$ is calculated to capture temporal dynamics.

Higher entropy implies maximum stochastic ambiguity. Figure \ref{fig:entropy_evolution} reveals two critical patterns:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Temporal Uncertainty Reduction:} Entropy decreases monotonically as the model accumulates evidence, signifying the system becomes increasingly certain about the hierarchy.
    \item \textbf{Polarization of Certainty:} A bifurcation exists where clear favorites/underdogs show rapidly decaying entropy while middle-tier contestants maintain higher levels, reflecting objective unpredictability.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Task 2: Counterfactual Fairness Assessment}
\label{sec:task2}

\subsection{Problem Analysis and Simulation Architecture}
Leveraging the posterior-latent-fan vote $X_t$, Task-2 constructs a Counterfactual-Causal Inference Framework to quantify the non-linear structural heterogeneity affecting elimination outcomes. Two theoretical imperatives are addressed:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A dynamic ``Theoretical-Survival-Cycle" logic must be established to handle data invalidation caused by such causal path-branching.
    \item The non-deterministic judge's \textit{Save} is parameterized as a probabilistic process driven by subjective latent variables, thereby defining the system's stochastic fairness boundaries.
\end{itemize}

To address the aforementioned challenges, a Two-Tier Simulation Architecture is proposed:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Historical Reconstruction Layer (Layer I):} By specifically modifying likelihood-constraint boundaries, Monte Carlo simulations are executed across historical time steps to reconstruct the counterfactual elimination sequence $E'$.
  
  \item \textbf{Probabilistic Intervention Simulation Layer (Layer II):} The non-deterministic \emph{Judge's Save} is modeled via a Sigmoid Logistic Response Function. This parameterizes the intervention as a stochastic process governed by a Rationality Coefficient, enabling quantitative assessment of non-linear impacts on fairness.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Mathematical Formalization and Fairness Evaluation System}
To unify heterogeneous competitive formats and evaluate their equity, we first formulate explicit decision functions for contestant $i$ in week $t$. Let $J_{i,t}$ denote the normalized judge score and $x_{i,t}$ the latent fan vote.

\textbf{Rank-Based Logic} maps continuous scores to discrete ranks $\mathcal{R}(\cdot)$ where larger values indicate worse performance. The total score $S_{i,t}^{Rank}$ and the elimination condition following the maximization principle are defined as:

\vspace{-0.4cm}
\begin{equation}
    % \vspace{-0.2cm}
    S_{i,t}^{Rank} = \mathcal{R}(J_{i,t}) + \mathcal{R}(x_{i,t}); \quad \mathbb{I}(E_{i,t}=1) \iff i = \arg\max_{k} S_{k,t}^{Rank}
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Percentage-Based Logic} preserves variable continuity on the simplex. With judge weight $w=0.5$, the composite score $S_{i,t}^{Pct}$ and the elimination condition following the minimization principle are:

\vspace{-0.4cm}
\begin{equation}
    S_{i,t}^{Pct} = w \cdot \frac{J_{i,t}}{\sum_{k} J_{k,t}} + (1-w) \cdot x_{i,t}; \quad \mathbb{I}(E_{i,t}=1) \iff i = \arg\min_{k} S_{k,t}^{Pct}
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
\end{equation}

Further quantifying the frequency of these anomalies, we define the \textbf{Absolute Injustice Rate} denoted as AIR and the \textbf{Relative Injustice Rate} denoted as RIR:

\vspace{-0.4cm}
\begin{equation}
    AIR = \frac{1}{N_{total}} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \mathbb{I}(J_{elim}^{(t)} > \bar{J}_{surv}^{(t)}), \quad
    RIR = \frac{1}{N_{diff}} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \mathbb{I}(J_{elim}^{(t)} > \bar{J}_{surv}^{(t)})
    \vspace{-0.1cm}
\end{equation}
$N_{total}$ represents the total number of weeks across all seasons while $N_{diff}$ denotes the subset of weeks where the elimination result was altered under counterfactual conditions.

Finally, conceptualizing weekly elimination as a Social Choice event, we define the \textbf{Total Utility Function} $U$ to evaluate comprehensive performance:

\vspace{-0.4cm}
\begin{equation}
    U_{total} = w_{pro} \cdot \bar{J}_{norm} + w_{pop} \cdot X - w_{pen} \cdot I
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
\end{equation}
$I$ denotes the smoothed Injustice Rate. This function balances the dual objectives of preserving technical excellence and maintaining popularity while explicitly penalizing unfair outcomes.

\subsection{Case Study: Structural Tolerance Analysis of Anomalous Samples}
Addressing specific samples in historical data that exhibit significant evaluation variance (e.g., \textit{Bobby Bones} in Season 27), we introduce a Survival Analysis framework. The objective is to measure the "Theoretical Survival Boundary" of contestants within different format spaces, thereby quantifying the sensitivity of distinct discriminative mechanisms to specific feature distributions.

% \subsubsection{Definition of Theoretical Survival Boundary}
We define the Theoretical Survival Upper Bound $L_{i}^{\mathcal{M}}$ for contestant $i$ under a specific discriminative mechanism $\mathcal{M}$ (e.g., Rank or Percentage). This variable represents the first time step in the counterfactual inference where the contestant satisfies the elimination condition:

\vspace{-0.2cm}
\begin{equation}
    L_{i}^{\mathcal{M}} = \min \{ t \mid \mathcal{D}(i,t | \mathcal{M}) = \text{Eliminated} \}
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{D}(\cdot)$ is the elimination state indicator constructed based on the aforementioned decision functions $S_{i,t}$.

% \subsubsection{Survival Gap Measure}
By comparing observational data with counterfactual results via Monte Carlo simulation, the model identifies a significant \textbf{Survival Gap ($\Delta_{Gap}$)}. This metric is defined as the difference between the contestant's observed survival duration in history and their theoretical survival upper bound:

\vspace{-0.2cm}
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{Gap} = L_{i}^{Observed} - L_{i}^{\mathcal{M}}
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
\end{equation}
The magnitude of $\Delta_{Gap}$ quantitatively characterizes the structural tolerance of the current format topology towards specific contestant types.

\subsection{Probabilistic Modeling of the "Judges' Save" Intervention}
For the non-deterministic "Judges' Save" mechanism introduced in Season 28, the model ceases to view it as a static logical judgment. Instead, we construct it as a probability distribution driven by latent discriminative logic. We employ a \textbf{Logistic Regression} framework to fit the decision boundary of judges within the endangered set (Bottom 2).

Assume the endangered set $\Omega_{bottom}$ contains two pending contestants $A$ and $B$, with explicit scores $J_A$ and $J_B$ respectively. The model posits that the judges' decision to "Save A" follows a Bernoulli distribution, where the probability parameter is determined by a non-linear mapping of the capability difference via a Sigmoid activation function:

\vspace{-0.2cm}
\vspace{-0.2cm}
\begin{equation}
    P(Save_{A} | J_{A}, J_{B}) = \sigma(\beta \cdot \Delta J) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp(-\beta \cdot (J_{A} - J_{B}))}
\end{equation}
Here, $\beta$ is defined as the \textbf{Rationality Coefficient}, a critical hyperparameter determining the entropy of the system.

We construct a Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA) model to quantify the trade-off between correcting technical injustice and respecting public democratic choice. We define the \textbf{Technical Merit Gain} (Benefit) and \textbf{Fan Disenfranchisement Cost} (Cost), and deriving the critical \textbf{Efficiency Ratio} (ER) as follows:

\vspace{-0.2cm}
\vspace{-0.2cm}
\begin{equation}
    \text{Benefit} = J_{saved} - J_{eliminated}, \quad \text{Cost} = F_{eliminated} - F_{saved}, \quad ER = \frac{\text{Benefit}}{\text{Cost}}
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
\end{equation}
An intervention is deemed efficient if and only if the technical gain sufficiently outweighs the cost of public disenfranchisement.

\subsection{Feature Space Construction}
DWTS relies on a dual-source mechanism: Judges (prioritizing objective skills) and the Audience (influenced by subjective popularity and 'anchoring effects'). Since direct correlation analysis obscures this heterogeneity, we construct independent models to quantify the differential feature impacts on Judge Scores ($S_{judge}$) and Fan Votes ($V_{fan}$).

To reduce the dimensionality of the high-cardinality "Pro Partner" feature while retaining information, we performed feature engineering. Instead of using one-hot encoding for individual partners, we extracted three core competency metrics: \textit{Average Historical Rank}, \textit{Previous Seasons Participated}, and \textit{Previous Championships Won}.
Consequently, the feature space is partitioned into two distinct sets:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Static Attribute Features ($X_{stat}$):} Includes Contestant Age, Industry Category (5 classes), and the three engineered Partner Competency features.
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Performance Features ($X_{perf}$):} Includes the Judge Score ($S_{judge}$) of the current week and the Predicted Fan Vote Share ($V_{fan}$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The Three-Tier XGBoost Architecture}
Traditional linear regression fails to capture high-order interactions (e.g., the non-linear decay of physical capability with age, or the synergistic effect between industry type and partner skill) \cite{4}. To address this, we employ \textbf{eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost)}, an ensemble framework based on decision trees. We minimize the regularized objective function $\mathcal{L}(\phi)$:

\vspace{-0.2cm}
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}(\phi) = \sum_{i} l(\hat{y}_{i}, y_{i}) + \sum_{k} \Omega(f_{k})
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
\end{equation}

Here, $l(\cdot)$ is the differentiable convex loss function, and $\Omega(f_k) = \gamma T + \frac{1}{2}\lambda ||w||^2$ is the regularization term controlling tree complexity (leaves $T$ and weights $w$).

We establish three independent models to isolate the specific drivers of judges, audience, and survival:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Model A (Judge Preference):} A regression model fitting $S_{judge} = f_A(X_{stat})$, aiming to isolate how static attributes impact technical scoring.
    \item \textbf{Model B (Audience Preference):} A regression model fitting $V_{fan} = f_B(X_{stat}, S_{judge})$, aiming to analyze the joint influence of static identity and judge guidance on public voting.
    \item \textbf{Model C (Survival Analysis):} A binary classification model predicting promotion status, minimizing the Log-Loss: $\mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\sum_{i} [y_{i}\log(\hat{y}_{i}) + (1-y_{i})\log(1-\hat{y}_{i})]$
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Game-Theoretic Interpretability via SHAP Architecture}
Although XGBoost excels in handling non-linear data, as an ensemble tree model, it lacks the intuitive interpretability of linear regression. To resolve this "Black Box" nature, we integrate the \textbf{SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations)} framework. Based on cooperative game theory, SHAP provides a unified measure of feature importance by attributing the prediction output to the marginal contribution of each feature.

\subsubsection{Mathematical Definition of Shapley Value}
SHAP conceptualizes the model prediction as a multi-player cooperative game:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Players:} The set of input features $F = \{Age, Industry, \dots\}$.
    \item \textbf{Payout:} The model's prediction output $f(x)$.
\end{itemize}

For a specific sample $x_i$, the SHAP value $\phi_j(x_i)$ for feature $j$ represents its \textbf{Marginal Contribution}. To strictly allocate contribution, we calculate the expected marginal contribution of feature $j$ across all possible feature subsets $S$:

\vspace{-0.2cm}
\begin{equation}
    \phi_{j}(x_{i}) = \sum_{S \subseteq F \setminus \{j\}} \frac{|S|!(|F|-|S|-1)!}{|F|!} [f(S \cup \{j\}) - f(S)]
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
\end{equation}

In this formulation, the term $[f(S \cup \{j\}) - f(S)]$ quantifies the marginal contribution of feature $j$ to the model's prediction relative to a subset $S$, while the coefficient $\frac{|S|!(|F|-|S|-1)!}{|F|!}$ serves as a combinatorial weight to ensure that all possible permutations of feature coalitions are treated equally in the attribution process.

\subsubsection{Local Additivity and Global Importance Mechanism}

To quantify the heterogeneous influence of features on survival outcomes, we leverage the SHAP framework. This method provides a unified measure of feature importance by attributing the prediction output to the marginal contribution of each feature within a game-theoretic structure. Our analysis relies on two core properties:

\vspace{-0.2cm}
\vspace{-0.2cm}
\begin{equation}
    f(x_i) = \phi_0 + \sum_{j=1}^{M} \phi_j(x_i)
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_0$ is the base value and $\phi_j(x_i)$ represents the specific contribution of feature $j$. This allows us to trace precisely how attributes like Age or Judge Score shift survival probability. Simultaneously, to derive a holistic hierarchy, we define the global importance $I_j$ as the mean absolute SHAP value:

\vspace{-0.2cm}
\vspace{-0.2cm}
\begin{equation}
    I_j = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} |\phi_j(x_i)|
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Causal Mediation Analysis Framework}
One innovation of this study is the application of \textbf{Causal Mediation Analysis}. In analyzing the determinants of survival, it is crucial to distinguish whether a feature affects the outcome by  degrading technical performance, or by inducing inherent prejudice. We construct two contrastive models based on the Survival Analysis architecture to calculate the \textbf{Skill Translation Rate}.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Model 1 (Total Effect Model):} Inputs only static features $X_{stat}$. This model captures the total causal influence of a feature on survival: $I_{j}^{total} = \text{Importance of } j \text{ in } P(\text{Safe} | X_{stat})$
    
    \item \textbf{Model 2 (Direct Effect Model):} Inputs both $X_{stat}$ and dynamic performance features $X_{perf}$. Here, $X_{perf}$ acts as a \textbf{Mediator}, encapsulating the influence related to a contestant's professional technical ability: $I_{j}^{direct} = \text{Importance of } j \text{ in } P(\text{Safe} | X_{stat}, X_{perf})$
\end{itemize}

We define the \textbf{Skill Translation Rate ($\lambda_j$)} to quantify the proportion of a feature's influence that is mediated by dynamic performance:

\vspace{-0.2cm}\vspace{-0.2cm}
\begin{equation}
    \lambda_{j} = \frac{I_{j}^{total} - I_{j}^{direct}}{I_{j}^{total}} \times 100\%
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item If $\lambda_j \approx 100\%$: The feature's impact is realized entirely through the contestant's individual capability (No Bias).
    \item If $\lambda_j \le 0$: The feature's importance increases after controlling for dynamic performance, suggesting that the feature primarily influences the result through inherent bias.
\end{itemize}

\section{Task 4: Optimization of the Scoring Architecture}
\label{sec:task4}

\subsection{Systemic Challenges and The FEHSS Framework}
In optimizing the DWTS format, the explicit variable is the Judge Score ($J$), while the Fan Vote Distribution ($X$) is an estimated latent variable. Both are heavily confounded by non-competitive factors like age and partner capability. This confounding obscures the unbiased latent variable—the "True Competitive Level"—causing the evaluation system to deviate from fairness. From a modeling perspective, constructing a fair system thus presents two fundamental difficulties:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{High Collinearity of Background Factors.} Variables like age negatively correlate with judge scores, and audience voting follows judge guidance. This requires the model to orthogonalize the feature space, decoupling the background-determined "Trend Component" from the effort-reflecting "Residual Component."
    
    \item \textbf{Non-Stationary Multi-Objective Balance.} Completely removing background factors may reduce commercial appeal. Thus, the model must dynamically balance "Competitive Fairness" and "Commercial Spectacle," necessitating adaptive weight parameters that evolve throughout the competition schedule.
\end{itemize}

Based on these challenges, we propose a \textbf{Fairness-Enhanced Hybrid Scoring System (FEHSS)}. This system uses statistical learning models to establish a benchmark expectation, employs residual analysis to strip away background factors, and introduces a time-decay mechanism to reconstruct the comprehensive score, thereby maintaining commercial viability.

\subsection{Statistical Learning-Based Background Decoupling Mechanism}
In this model, the Observed Score of a contestant is defined as a non-linear superposition of an explicit "Background Trend Component" and an implicit "Competitive Residual Component." To acquire the true competitive level, the model utilizes the predictors constructed in Task 3 to quantify and decouple the background trend.

\subsubsection{Construction of Non-Linear Expectation Benchmarks}
Research in Task 3 indicated that the influence of background variables on results exhibits significant non-linearity. Therefore, we utilize the \textbf{XGBoost} framework to build the expectation benchmark model.

Based on SHAP value contribution analysis, we construct two independent benchmark predictors:

\textbf{A. Expected Technical Score Model ($E[J_i]$)}
This model aims to predict the theoretical technical score achievable considering only physiological limits and resource allocation. Its mathematical form is defined as:

\vspace{-0.2cm}
\begin{equation}
    E[J_i] = \mathcal{F}_{XGB}^A(X_{stat}) = \mathcal{F}_{XGB}^A(\text{Age}_i, \text{PartnerRank}_i, \text{Industry}_i, \dots)
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
\end{equation}
SHAP analysis shows that age contributes 55.5\% to the marginal contribution of judge scores, followed by the partner's historical average rank at 17.7\%. $E[J_i]$ reflects the "Base Score" a contestant can attain given physiological decline and auxiliary resource differences determined by the partner.

\textbf{B. Expected Popularity Model ($E[V_i]$)}
This model aims to predict the theoretical audience votes under the judge guidance effect and professional dividends. Its form is:

\vspace{-0.2cm}\vspace{-0.2cm}
\begin{equation}
    E[V_i] = \mathcal{F}_{XGB}^B(X_{stat}, J_i) = \mathcal{F}_{XGB}^B(J_i, \text{IsAthlete}_i, \dots)
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
\end{equation}

To characterize the non-stationary evolution of the DWTS evaluation standard—focusing on professional capability in the early stages and popularity in the later stages—we define the technical weight parameter $\alpha(t)$ to follow an exponential decay distribution:

\end{equation}
The mathematical properties of the geometric mean determine its high sensitivity to minimum values. If a contestant's score in either dimension approaches zero, their total score will sharply converge to zero. 

This aggregation method mathematically forces contestants to pursue the joint maximization of $J_{adj}$ and $V_{adj}$. Compared to the arithmetic mean, the geometric mean severely penalizes "Single-Dimension Dependency" strategies, ensuring that advancing contestants are robust in both technique and popularity.

\section{Conclusion}

This study developed a robust predictive framework integrating Variational Inference and Bayesian recursive updates. By employing Monte Carlo rejection sampling, the model achieved a 99.66\% Top-1 accuracy and a Posterior Concentration Index of 0.9864 across 34 seasons, effectively capturing time-varying popularity dynamics with high statistical certainty.

Comparative analysis of competition structures reveals that the Rank-based system superiorly suppresses voting noise and protects technical merit through ordinal processing, whereas the Percentage-based format increases the risk of systemic injustice. Furthermore, stochastic interventions like the Judges' Save mechanism are found to be institutionally legitimate only when governed by rigorous, capability-based thresholds.

Feature attribution via XGBoost and SHAP demonstrates that over 80\% of the impact from celebrity age and partner expertise is mediated indirectly through technical scores. Notably, audience influence exerts a decision weight nearly four times that of professional evaluations, with distinct feature sensitivities observed between the public and expert judges.

To address these imbalances, we proposed the Fairness-Enhanced Hybrid Scoring System (FEHSS). By utilizing residual decoupling and time-varying geometric weighting, FEHSS minimizes single-dimension speculation and dynamically balances competitive integrity with commercial entertainment value, significantly enhancing the stability and rationality of the competition format.

\textbf{Query}: “We are modeling the latent fan vote shares as a Dirichlet distribution since they sum to 1. However, we are struggling to formulate the likelihood function for the inverse problem where only the rank-based elimination result is observed. Could you propose a mathematical framework to define the posterior probability $P(\text{Votes} | \text{Elimination})$ using Bayesian inference?”

\vspace{0.1cm}
\textbf{Output}: 
To address the inverse problem of reconstructing latent fan votes from observed elimination results, we can formulate a Bayesian framework where the elimination outcome acts as a hard constraint on the simplex space. Here is the proposed mathematical formulation:

\subsubsection*{1. Prior Distribution}
Let $\mathbf{v}_t = (v_1, v_2, \dots, v_n)$ represent the vector of fan vote shares for $n$ contestants at week $t$. Since $\sum v_i = 1$, we assume a Dirichlet prior parameterized by $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$:
\[
\mathbf{v}_t \sim \text{Dirichlet}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
\]
where $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$ can be uniform ($\alpha_i = 1$) for an uninformative prior or weighted based on previous weeks.

\subsubsection*{2. Likelihood Function (Hard Constraint)}
The observation $E_{obs}$ (e.g., "Contestant $k$ was eliminated") is a deterministic outcome of the aggregation rule $R(\mathbf{v}_t, \mathbf{s}_t)$, where $\mathbf{s}_t$ denotes the known judges' scores. The likelihood is therefore an indicator function $\mathbb{I}(\cdot)$:
\[
P(E_{obs} | \mathbf{v}_t, \mathbf{s}_t) = \mathbb{I}\left( \text{Rule}(\mathbf{v}_t, \mathbf{s}_t) \to E_{obs} \right)
\]
This function equals 1 if the vote vector $\mathbf{v}_t$ combined with $\mathbf{s}_t$ results in the observed elimination, and 0 otherwise.

\subsubsection*{3. Posterior Distribution}
Applying Bayes' Theorem, the posterior probability density is:
\[
P(\mathbf{v}_t | E_{obs}) = \frac{P(E_{obs} | \mathbf{v}_t) \cdot P(\mathbf{v}_t)}{\int P(E_{obs} | \mathbf{v}) P(\mathbf{v}) d\mathbf{v}} \propto \mathbb{I}(\text{Consistent}) \cdot \text{Dirichlet}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
\]
This represents a \textbf{Truncated Dirichlet Distribution}, defined only over the convex polytope within the simplex where the rank constraints hold.

\subsubsection*{4. Numerical Solution: Rejection Sampling}
Since the normalization constant (the integral over the polytope) is analytically intractable, we recommend a Monte Carlo simulation approach:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Sample:} Draw a candidate vector $\mathbf{v}^{(i)} \sim \text{Dirichlet}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})$.
    \item \textbf{Evaluate:} Compute the total scores using $\mathbf{v}^{(i)}$ and $\mathbf{s}_t$ under the specific season's rules (Rank vs. Percentage).
    \item \textbf{Filter:} Accept $\mathbf{v}^{(i)}$ if it strictly reproduces the historical elimination result $E_{obs}$; otherwise, reject it.
\end{enumerate}
The collection of accepted samples $\{\mathbf{v}^{(1)}, \dots, \mathbf{v}^{(M)}\}$ serves as an empirical approximation of the posterior distribution $P(\mathbf{v}_t | E_{obs})$.

import numpy as np

def calculate_survival_vectorized(judge_scores, fan_vote_samples):
    """
    judge_scores: array of shape (n_contestants,)
    fan_vote_samples: matrix of shape (n_simulations, n_contestants)
    """
    # 1. Broadcasting Addition
    # Dimensions: (1, N) + (Sims, N) -> (Sims, N)
    total_scores = judge_scores[None, :] + fan_vote_samples

    # 2. Vectorized Ranking
    # argsort twice transforms scores into 0-indexed ranks
    # axis=1 performs ranking within each simulation independently
    ranks = np.argsort(np.argsort(total_scores, axis=1), axis=1)

    # 3. Boolean Mask for Survival
    # Assuming Bottom 2 are at risk (ranks 0 and 1)
    # The condition returns a boolean matrix of shape (Sims, N)
    is_safe_matrix = (ranks >= 2)

    # 4. Compute Survival Rate
    # Averaging along axis 0 (across simulations)
    survival_rates = np.mean(is_safe_matrix, axis=0)

    return survival_rates
\end{verbatim}

\textit{Performance Note:} This approach reduces the complexity from iterative $O(K \cdot N)$ to a vectorized operation that leverages BLAS optimization, resulting in a speedup of approximately $100\times$ for $10,000$ iterations.
